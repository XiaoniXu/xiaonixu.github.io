About Me

I’m a healthcare data scientist and a Research Assistant at Cedars‑Sinai in Los Angeles, where I build clinical AI and machine‑learning systems, from LLM‑based phenotyping agents to multimodal deep learning on breast MRI for predicting treatment response.

I earned my M.S. in Biostatistics from Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health (2026), where I worked with Professors Linda Valeri and Todd Ogden on causal mediation analysis, functional data analysis, and other methods that bridge statistics and public‑health applications. I’ve also analyzed Phase 3 clinical trial data with Bristol Myers Squibb and IQVIA.

Before Columbia, I earned a B.A. in Biology (Ecology & Evolution) from the University of Chicago. Under the mentorship of Professors Graham Slater and John Bates at the Field Museum, I investigated 3‑D morphometric evolution of avian cranial structures and contributed to specimen‑based molecular and morphometric projects.

My broader interests span clinical AI, causal inference, survival analysis, functional data analysis, animal morphometrics, and ecological and environmental systems. Outside of work, you’ll find me birding and exploring cities via all kinds of public transit.
