Causal Mediation Analysis with Longitudinal Mediators & Competing Risks

My work in the Valeri Lab (2025–2026) focused on extending the CMAverse R package. I have developed and implemented a user‑friendly command, cmest_pathcomprisk, which automates causal mediation analysis when (i) mediators are measured repeatedly over time and (ii) both mediators and outcomes are subject to competing events such as death.

Diseases of aging—e.g. dementia—often face the competing risk of mortality. Ignoring this alters estimates of indirect effects. Our command provides path‑specific effect estimates that remain causally interpretable under standard identifiability assumptions. I validated this function through extensive simulations, shipped a full testing suite and implementation guide, and applied it to 5,000+ longitudinal patient observations to study aging‑related health outcomes.

Functional Data Analysis of Mitochondrial Morphology

Under Prof. Todd Ogden, I built an automated image‑processing pipeline for 650+ biological objects (mitochondrial and egg morphology) and applied functional data analysis tools (MHD, fdasrvf) to 3‑D mitochondrial shape trajectories in running vs. sedentary mice. We quantify shape variability along geodesic paths, using distance matrices to relate morphological features to metabolic phenotypes. Avian Cranial Evolution

I worked with Rossy Natale, Prof. Graham Slater, and Prof. John Bates to examine the semi‑independent evolution of bird beaks and braincases using high-density 3-D landmarks from shorebirds (order Charadriiformes). We compared 11 alternative “modularity” models. We found that the beak has followed its own semi-independent evolutionary path while remaining moderately correlated with the rest of the skull. This research project has been presented at the 2022 American Ornithological Society Annual Conference in Puerto Rico.
